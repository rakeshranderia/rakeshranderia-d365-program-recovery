# D365 Program Recovery & Business Process Improvement

> Organisation and identifying details intentionally omitted.

## Context

A growing organisation was working through a Microsoft Dynamics 365 transformation intended to support customer, operational and business-process improvement.

The program had experienced earlier implementation difficulties and required stabilisation before delivery could proceed effectively.

## Challenge

The problem was not simply technical.

There were gaps between:

- business expectations;
- system design;
- delivery ownership;
- integration;
- vendor accountability;
- operational readiness.

Recovery therefore required more than another configuration exercise.

The program needed clearer business ownership, delivery discipline and alignment between the platform and the processes it was intended to support.

## Approach

Business stakeholders, internal technology teams and delivery partners were brought together to reset the program around business outcomes.

The work included:

- clarifying requirements and process ownership;
- defining scope and acceptance criteria;
- reviewing solution design;
- strengthening vendor accountability;
- establishing delivery governance and escalation;
- reviewing integration dependencies;
- improving testing and operational readiness;
- planning adoption, training and transition to BAU.

Dynamics 365 was treated as one component of a wider business and technology ecosystem rather than an isolated application.

Particular attention was given to ensuring that system decisions reflected the end-to-end business process instead of optimising individual components in isolation.

## Business Process & Integration

The platform supported customer-facing and operational processes that crossed organisational and technology boundaries.

Recovery therefore considered:

- customer and service workflows;
- business hand-offs;
- surrounding enterprise systems;
- data movement;
- reporting;
- security and access;
- operational support.

Integration was treated as part of the business process rather than a purely technical concern.

## Delivery Governance

A more structured delivery model established:

- clear decision rights;
- accountable business ownership;
- vendor responsibilities;
- RAID management;
- escalation paths;
- readiness checkpoints;
- testing and acceptance criteria;
- communication and stakeholder forums.

The objective was visibility and accountability rather than additional bureaucracy.

## Testing, Adoption & Go-Live

Delivery included structured validation across business and operational requirements.

This included:

- progressive business demonstrations;
- UAT;
- operational readiness testing;
- defect triage and retesting;
- role-based user training;
- communications;
- cutover planning;
- go / no-go criteria;
- initial production support.

## Hypercare & BAU Transition

Go-live was treated as a transition point rather than the end of the program.

The stabilisation period included:

- structured incident and defect triage;
- monitoring of key processes and integrations;
- user support;
- vendor coordination;
- documentation refinement;
- knowledge transfer.

BAU ownership was defined across platform administration, support, integrations, change, reporting and supplier management.

## Outcome

The program moved from a recovery position toward a more structured and sustainable delivery model.

The most important improvements were:

- clearer accountability;
- stronger business engagement;
- improved vendor ownership;
- better alignment between process and technology;
- more disciplined delivery and operational transition.

## Key Lesson

> ERP and CRM recovery programs are rarely fixed by changing technology alone.

Sustainable recovery usually requires restoring:

**Business ownership + delivery discipline + process clarity + vendor accountability**

The technology then becomes an enabler of that operating model rather than the program itself.
